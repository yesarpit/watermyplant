How We Choose the Gardeners Who Water Your Plants

TL;DR (Quick Answer): Gardeners who join us through our for-gardeners page write to us first. Before we send them a job we check where they work, how much notice they need and what they charge per visit. We see an ID, and we speak to someone they have worked for. We're adding gardeners city by city, and this process is new: if we don't have a gardener near you yet, we tell you before anything is booked.

Why this matters

Someone you've never met is coming into your home, or at least onto your balcony, while you're away. You should know how they got there.

Step 1: the gardener asks us first

Gardeners and nurseries who want work write to us on WhatsApp from our for-gardeners page. Gardeners who reach us this way join our list only after they write to us and then confirm with a "yes" when we reply. We use their number only for plant-care jobs, and a customer gets it only once the gardener has agreed to that customer's job. They can reply STOP at any time, and we won't call or message them again.

Step 2: five things we check before the first job

  1. Where they work: their pincode and how far they'll travel, so we can send you someone nearby.
  2. When they're free: at least three days a week, with no more than two days' notice.
  3. What they charge: one clear rate per visit. That rate is the gardener's, and our fee is added on top, not taken out of it.
  4. An ID: we see an ID, such as a government ID or a nursery registration. We write down only which kind of ID we saw, never its number.
  5. A reference: we speak to one person they've worked for, such as a past customer, a nursery owner or an RWA.

A gardener who joins this way gets no job from us until all five checks pass.

Step 3: what you see before you book

The gardener's name, the dates, the number of visits and the full price. Nothing is booked until you agree.

What we won't claim

We're new. We don't have a gardener in every city yet, and we won't pretend to. Tell us your city and dates, and if there's no gardener near you, we'll say so.
