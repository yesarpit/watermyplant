Jagajyothi Layout sits within Bangalore's broader climate: mild year-round with two monsoon spells rather than one. That's kinder to houseplants than most Indian cities, but it still means your watering routine should shift with the season, not stay fixed all year.

Bangalore's plant seasons, in short

  • March-May (dry, warm): Soil dries out fastest this time of year. Check moisture every 1-2 days for potted plants in direct sun; balcony plants may need daily watering.
  • June-September (southwest monsoon): Rain does a lot of the work for outdoor/balcony plants. Cut back manual watering and check that pots drain well — waterlogged roots are the bigger risk than drought in this stretch.
  • October-November (northeast monsoon): A second, often heavier rain spell for Bangalore. Same drainage caution as above, especially after back-to-back rainy days.
  • December-February (cool, dry): Growth slows for most houseplants. Watering once every 4-7 days is usually enough indoors — overwatering in this cooler stretch is a common cause of root rot.

Common Bangalore apartment plants

Money plants, snake plants, ZZ plants, and areca palms are common in Bangalore homes because they tolerate the city's moderate light and humidity swings well. Money plants and snake plants in particular can go longer between waterings than most guides (written for hotter, drier cities) suggest — when in doubt, check the top inch of soil before adding water rather than watering on a fixed schedule.
