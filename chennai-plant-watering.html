Chennai's plant seasons, in short

  • April-June (peak heat): The hottest stretch, with 38-42°C days in May. Humidity is high but pots in sun still dry out daily.
  • July-September (hot, occasional storms): Chennai gets only part of the southwest monsoon: evening thunderstorms between hot, humid days. Keep watering, but check the soil first.
  • October-December (northeast monsoon): Chennai's main rainy season, with heavy spells and sometimes cyclones. Drainage and wind are the concerns; outdoor pots need little watering.
  • January-March (mildest): Warm days, slightly cooler nights, little rain. The easiest months; water indoor plants every 3-5 days.

Going away from Chennai?

  • 3-day trip: in January-March, water well and group pots out of direct sun. In April-June, small sunny pots need a visit or a move indoors.
  • 1 week: 3-4 visits in April-June; 2 in July-September and January-March; in the October-December rains, 1 visit for indoor and covered pots, plus emptying saucers.
  • 2 weeks or more: 6-7 visits in the hot months, 2-4 the rest of the year.

Balcony and terrace pots in Chennai

Near the coast, in Besant Nagar, Adyar, Thiruvanmiyur and along ECR, salty sea air scorches leaf edges; rinse leaves with fresh water every week or two. Many homes rely on borewell or tanker water that can be hard or slightly saline, which builds up in pots over time, so flush pots with plenty of water now and then. Before the northeast monsoon, tie climbers down, bring hanging baskets in and move tall pots away from the balcony edge; cyclone winds break more plants than the rain does. Constant humidity suits money plants, crotons, palms, jasmine (malli) and hibiscus, but it also suits mealybugs, so check under leaves regularly.
