How to Keep Plants Alive While You Travel: 1 Week, 2 Weeks or a Month

TL;DR (Quick Answer): For a trip of up to about 5 days, most healthy potted plants survive if you water deeply, move them out of direct sun and group them together. For 1-2 weeks, add a self-watering setup and plan at least one visit in hot months. For a month, someone needs to visit: once a week in cool or rainy months, every 2-3 days in an Indian summer.

The honest answer to "how long can my plants go without water?" is: it depends far more on the month and the pot than on the plant. A snake plant indoors in December can go three weeks; a tulsi in a small terracotta pot on a south-facing balcony in May may not last two days. So plan by season and by pot, not by plant name.

First, sort your pots into three groups

  • Tough: succulents, cacti, jade, snake plant, ZZ plant, aloe. Fine for 2-3 weeks indoors in most months.
  • Average: money plant (pothos), rubber plant, areca palm, spider plant, most crotons. About a week between waterings indoors; less on a sunny balcony.
  • Thirsty: tulsi, hibiscus, herbs, ferns, peace lily, flowering annuals, anything in a pot smaller than 6 inches. Two to four days in hot weather.

Your plan only has to work for the thirsty group; the others will be fine if the thirsty ones are.

Before any trip

  1. Water every pot deeply the evening before you leave, until water runs out of the drainage hole. Empty the saucers afterwards if it's the monsoon.
  2. Move pots out of direct afternoon sun. A spot with bright, indirect light slows drying a lot.
  3. Group pots together; they keep each other's air more humid.
  4. Switch off fans pointed at plants, and don't leave pots near an AC vent.
  5. Cover the soil of the thirstiest pots with a layer of mulch, coconut husk or even a few layers of damp newspaper.

A 1-week trip

In winter (roughly November-February) or the monsoon, most homes get through a week with the steps above, plus a self-watering bottle or wick for the thirsty group. In April-June, that isn't enough for balcony and terrace pots anywhere in North and Central India, and it's risky even in milder cities: small pots in sun need water every 1-2 days. Either bring them indoors into indirect light, or have someone come in 2-3 times.

Our guide to self-watering setups covers which ones actually last a week.

A 2-week trip

Two weeks is where DIY setups start to fail. Bottles empty, wicks dry out, and a spike set up for dry weather can drown a pot if the rain arrives. Plan on visits:

  • Hot, dry months: every 2-3 days, so 5-7 visits for sunny pots.
  • Monsoon: 2-3 visits, mostly for indoor and covered-balcony plants, and to empty saucers and check for rot.
  • Cool months: 2 visits are usually enough.

A month or longer

For a month, no self-watering setup is reliable on its own. Plan a visit at least once a week in cool or rainy months and every 2-3 days in summer. Leave a short note for whoever visits: which pots are thirsty, which should be left dry, and where the watering can is. Tell them about any plants that are meant to look dormant (adeniums and bougainvillea often drop leaves in the cold) so they don't overwater them to "fix" it.

How the season changes the plan, city by city

  • Delhi NCR: April-June heat (up to 45°C) is the danger period; winter trips are easy.
  • Mumbai: the monsoon brings too much water to open pots and none to enclosed balconies; the October heat dries pots quickly.
  • Bangalore: mild most of the year, but March-May is dry and warm and catches people out.
  • Hyderabad: April and May are hot and dry, often above 40°C, and balcony pots can need water every day; November-February trips are easy.
  • Pune: a steady June-September monsoon, then a dry, cool winter when pots dry slowly; March-May is hot enough to plan visits.
  • Chennai: warm all year, with the northeast monsoon in October-December, so a Diwali trip can mean heavy rain; May and June are the hardest months for pots.

When you get back

If a plant has wilted, water it once, thoroughly, and move it into shade; don't keep pouring water in over the next few days, because a stressed plant can't use it and the roots rot. Crisp brown leaves won't turn green again, but new growth usually comes within two weeks. If a pot smells sour or the soil stays wet for days, the problem was too much water, not too little: see the monsoon guide for what to do.
