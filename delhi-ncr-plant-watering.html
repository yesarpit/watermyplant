Delhi NCR's plant seasons, in short

  • April-June (extreme heat): Temperatures reach 42-46°C and the hot, dry loo wind blows in May and June. Small pots in sun can dry out twice a day; this is when a missed watering kills plants.
  • July-September (monsoon): Rain arrives around the end of June. Humidity jumps, growth is fast, and pots need less water but good drainage.
  • October-November (pleasant, smoggy): Mild days and cooler nights, but dust and smog coat leaves and cut the light they get. Water every 2-4 days and wipe leaves.
  • December-February (cold, foggy): Nights drop to 3-7°C, with dense fog in January. Growth nearly stops. Water sparingly and only in the morning; tropical plants may need to come indoors.

Going away from Delhi NCR?

  • 3-day trip: in May-June, even a short trip is risky for small pots in sun: move everything into shade, group it, and water deeply the night before. In winter, a 3-day trip needs nothing special.
  • 1 week: in April-June plan on a visit every 2 days (3-4 visits). In the monsoon and October-November, 2 visits. In December-February, 1 visit is usually enough.
  • 2 weeks or more: summer trips need 6-7 visits for pots in sun; the rest of the year, 2-4.

Terrace and balcony pots in Delhi NCR

Rooftop terraces in Delhi's builder floors and the upper balconies of Gurugram and Noida towers take the full afternoon sun in summer. A green shade net (50% is common) over the terrace from April to June cuts heat stress a lot; unglazed terracotta pots lose water fastest, so glazed or plastic pots, or a layer of mulch on top, help. Wipe or hose down leaves every week or two: the dust film from October to February blocks light. In the coldest weeks, move money plants, crotons and other tropical plants away from open balconies at night, and don't be alarmed when adeniums and bougainvilleas drop leaves; they're resting, and watering them more only rots the roots. Bougainvillea, jade, aloe, snake plants and hardy herbs like mint and curry leaf handle Delhi's swings best.
