Bangalore's plant seasons, in short

  • March-May (dry, warm): The hardest months for pots. Days reach 33-35°C with little rain, and small pots in direct sun can need water every day.
  • June-September (southwest monsoon): Steady but moderate rain. Outdoor pots get much of what they need from the sky; check drainage and water covered balconies as usual.
  • October-November (northeast monsoon): Often the heaviest downpours of the year arrive in short bursts. Waterlogged pots and fungus gnats are the common problems, not thirst.
  • December-February (cool, dry): Cool nights (around 15°C), dry air, slow growth. Indoor plants usually need water only every 4-7 days; overwatering is the main risk.

Going away from Bangalore?

How many visits a trip needs depends mostly on the month:

  • 3-day trip: most homes need no visit. Water well the evening before you leave, group pots together out of direct afternoon sun, and set a tray of damp pebbles under the thirstiest ones.
  • 1 week: in March-May plan on 2-3 visits for sunny terrace pots; in the monsoon and winter months, 1 visit is usually enough.
  • 2 weeks or more: 3-5 visits in the dry months, 2-3 the rest of the year. Self-watering spikes rarely last this long on their own.

Balcony and terrace pots in Bangalore

Apartment balconies in Whitefield, Sarjapur Road or Hebbal are often east- or west-facing and half covered, so rain reaches only the outer row of pots; the inner row needs watering even in the monsoon. Independent-house terraces in areas like Jayanagar, Basavanagudi or Kengeri get full sun and wind and dry out fastest in April. Bangalore's red soil and hard tap water in some areas leave a white crust on pots over time; flush pots with plenty of water once a month so salts don't build up. Hibiscus, tulsi, curry leaf and most flowering plants are the first to suffer when a watering is missed; snake plants, ZZ plants and succulents cope with a week on their own.
