Mumbai's plant seasons, in short

  • June-September (monsoon): Mumbai gets most of its more than 2,000 mm of yearly rain in these four months, heaviest in July. Open pots stay wet for weeks; enclosed balconies and window grills stay dry and still need watering.
  • October (the 'October heat'): Rain stops, humidity stays high and temperatures climb back to 33-35°C. Pots that were soaked all monsoon suddenly dry out fast.
  • November-February (mild, drier): The easiest months: warm days, cooler nights, low rain. Most indoor plants need water every 3-5 days.
  • March-May (hot, humid): Humid heat before the monsoon. Soil dries quickly in sunny spots, and mealybugs and spider mites spread on stressed plants.

Going away from Mumbai?

  • 3-day trip: water well before you leave and move pots out of direct sun. In the monsoon, move open-balcony pots under cover so they don't sit in rain for three days.
  • 1 week: in March-May and October, 2-3 visits for sunny pots. In the monsoon, open-air pots may need none, but plants inside the flat or in a closed balcony still need 1-2.
  • 2 weeks or more: 3-5 visits in the hot months, 2-3 in November-February. In the monsoon a visit is as much about emptying saucers and checking for rot as watering.

Window-grill and balcony pots in Mumbai

Most Mumbai plants live in window grills, narrow balconies or enclosed balconies that have been turned into rooms, and high-rise flats in Powai, Andheri or Thane often get only a few hours of direct sun. That means slower drying than people expect, and overwatering is the most common way plants die here. Near the sea (Bandra, Worli, Colaba, Juhu), salt-laden wind scorches leaf edges; rinse leaves with fresh water now and then. On high floors, monsoon winds knock over tall pots, so keep them low and wedged in. Use pots with drainage holes and raise them off the floor in June, because standing water in a saucer for a week is enough to rot the roots. Money plants, snake plants, pothos, peace lilies and areca palms suit Mumbai flats best.
